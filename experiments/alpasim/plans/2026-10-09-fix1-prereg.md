# FIX1: speed-continuous serving and an openpilot lead limit in the AlpaSim driver (pre-registration, 2026-10-09)

Lane FIX1. Written and pushed before any closed-loop score of a fixed arm is read. Serving-side only: no training, no new weights, no
simulator ground truth in the driver, nothing submitted. Code: `lib/serve_fix.py` (switches `JEV_VCONT`, `JEV_LEAD`, both off by default),
hooks in `sh30_driver.py` / `ap2_driver.py` / `pai_driver.py`, chain `scripts/fix1_chain.sh`, read-out `scripts/fix1_report.py`.

## Why (lane COL1's interim findings, 20 PAI scenes and the nuPlan collision taxonomy)

1. The returned trajectory is the plan's 0.5 s points resampled linearly, so its first segment has the plan's mean speed over 0.5 s whatever
   the ego's speed is. On PAI the step is -3 to -11 % above 16 m/s (two spins at the hand-over above 23 m/s) and +5 to +28 % at 5-12 m/s.
2. The frozen model's own lead outputs reported the struck lead seconds before impact in the lead collisions; the driver discards them.

## What is fixed here, and how it differs from settled mechanisms

**(a) `JEV_VCONT=1.0`, speed-continuous serving.** The path is unchanged; the speed profile starts at the ego's speed and blends linearly into
the plan's own segment speeds over 1.0 s. The value is read off the controller, not tuned: AlpaSim's nonlinear MPC (0bb4c4b, run as shipped)
has a 20 x 0.1 s horizon and costs the reference only from horizon index `idx_start_penalty` = 10, i.e. positions 1.0-2.0 s ahead (position
weights 2 / 1, acceleration weight 0.1, command-change weights 5 / 1, acceleration command in [-9, 6] m/s^2). A linear blend over that 1.0 s
is the reference that a constant acceleration (v_plan - v_ego) / 1 s meets with zero position and speed error at the first costed index.
It is the form of COL1's diagnostic arm `v1` (same function to 1e-9 m on random plans), which COL1 fixed before its first run; here it is
a candidate with lines. It keeps the plan's absolute speed profile after 1 s, so the adapter's speed prior (decisions 162 / 167: the
launch ability of P2H on this domain) stays; it is not the "adapter off at standstill" or "subtract the mean bias" of decision 167.

**(b) `JEV_LEAD=1`, lead-aware longitudinal limit.** openpilot's own path from the model's lead outputs to an acceleration limit, from
source (openpilot master ec95db3f, the commit `lib/op_ctrl.py` emulates): radard's asymmetric probability filter and vision-only lead
(dRel = x - camera-to-front-bumper, vLead = v_ego + lead v - the model's own ego speed), the lead MPC of `long_mpc.py` (cost on
(x_obstacle - x - d(v)) / (v + 10), d(v) = v^2 / 5 + 1.45 v + 6, x_obstacle = x_lead + v_lead^2 / 5, acceleration-change 200, jerk 5,
danger zone 0.75 d(v) at 100, limits [-3.5, 2.0] m/s^2, v >= 0), the planner's v_desired filter and `get_accel_from_plan`, output
min(lead MPC, e2e). Ticks at 20 Hz with the model's last output held (10 ticks per nuPlan decision, 2 per PAI decision). Lead source: the
served checkpoint's own output vector (free in the forward pass; a second pass with the shipped weights would add about 20 ms). The same
problem solved by scipy SLSQP instead of acados. Ours, because openpilot has no position-tracking interface: the served speed profile is the
pointwise minimum of the profile without the switch and the MPC's speed solution re-anchored at the ego speed. It only removes speed and has
no latch. Not emulated: radar, cruise candidate, FCW, LongControl's stopping state.

Differences from settled reads: decision 157 = a fixed 2 m margin subtracted from the lead distance, arc capped at g + v t - d_min, offline;
decision 140 = a resume rule that starts a standing car when no lead is seen (this switch never starts a car); decision 167 = switching the
adapter bias; `op_control_stack_long` (HUGSIM) = openpilot's longitudinal path driven by the action head, explicitly without "radar and the
MPC lead protection", which is exactly the part taken here; decisions 179 / 203 and COL1's guard G(p*, a*) are offline reads of the lead
signal (AUC, a threshold rule's ceiling: turned 6 / 22 with false-alarm scenes 0.14, line "not as a rule"). None put openpilot's following
law in a closed loop. COL1's ceiling is the prior here: at most 2 of the 12 at-fault collisions of P2H10-F-s0 on 1491 scenes are a stopped
lead, so on nuPlan the expected gain of (b) is a handful of scenes and its risk is slow scenes.

## Design reads made before this registration (no closed-loop score of any arm)

- Source: the AlpaSim controller, the LTF sample's `trajectory.py`, openpilot `radard.py`, `long_mpc.py`, `longitudinal_planner.py`,
  `drive_helpers.py`.
- Toy longitudinal scenarios of the MPC emulation against a converged solve (stopped / slower / departing lead, standstill).
- Open-loop replay (`scripts/fix1_replay.py`, the ego does not react) of the three arms on COL1's extracted nuPlan messages: the at-fault
  collision rollouts of P2H10-F-s0 / -s1 / APY10m10-AB-s0 and 200 control rollouts of P2H10-F-s0 (parts 001-002, inside the 700 scenes).
  Table: `results/fix1/replay.md`. What it showed, and what it changed:
  - Switches off: every plan identical to COL1's replay (max difference 0.0 m), so the hooks are inert.
  - (a) on nuPlan is small: the plan's first-segment speed is 0.98-0.99 x the ego's above 2 m/s (p10 0.88-0.96) and 1.12 x at 0.5-2 m/s;
    the switch moves the 2 s arc by a median +0.1 m (p10-p90 -0.3 to +0.3 m). Expected effect on the nuPlan score: about none.
  - The plan's own speed at t = 0 (the model's ego speed in openpilot's relative lead speed) is within 0.3 m/s of the ego's (median), so the
    relative form is kept as openpilot has it.
  - (b) would act widely: a lead is present at 1150 of 2000 control decisions and the limit removes >= 0.5 m of the first 2 s at 559
    (541 of them with no object in the corridor that needs braking), in 98 of 200 control scenes at two consecutive decisions; median
    1.3 m. On the collision rollouts it acts while a stop is still possible in 4 of 12 (P2H10-F-s0), 4 of 10 (-s1), 6 of 10 (APY10m10-AB-s0).
    openpilot's following distance is longer than the logged drivers' in nuPlan traffic: the slow-count line is expected to fail for the
    arms with (b) on nuPlan. Nothing was changed to avoid that (no constant of openpilot's was touched); the closed loop decides.
  - Cost: (a) 0.15 ms per decision; (b) 16.7 ms median (10 ticks), with a 190 ms first call. Changed before this registration, for latency
    only: scipy is imported at start-up and the SLSQP tolerance is 1e-6 instead of 1e-9 (same closed-loop gaps on the toy cases, about half
    the time on easy ticks). No behavioural constant was changed.
- On PAI nothing beyond COL1's committed descriptions was looked at. The baseline of the last 20 scenes (`b2a`, `b2b`) and COL1's `v1`
  runs on scenes other than `a10` had not been read by this lane when this file was pushed.

## nuPlan read (GPU box, native stack, pool jobs through `ot2_loop.py`, OT_LANE=fix1)

Stage 1: the 700 public scenes with OT3's three chunk lists (`c0b/lists/chunk{0,1,2}.txt`), all four arms at the same checkout. The base is
re-run rather than taken from OT3 so that arm and base share code, settings and scene lists; OT3's P2H10-F-s0 run is shown beside it as the
run-to-run difference of the simulator. Reported per arm: mean scene score, zeros by flag (at-fault collision / offroad / left corridor),
slow count (0 < score < 1), at-fault events, paired difference against the base with scene-resampled and log-clustered 95 % bootstrap
intervals (10 000 draws, seed 0), zero <-> non-zero flips, decisions and scenes in which the lead limit removed >= 0.5 m of the first 2 s.
Also +a+b against +a and against +b (attribution).

Lines, each arm against the base:

| Line | Reading |
|---|---|
| at-fault-collision zeros drop **and** difference >= 0 **and** log-clustered lower bound > -0.005 **and** slow count <= 1.1 x base | the arm is **kept** |
| kept and difference >= +0.005 with log-clustered lower bound > 0 | **gain** |
| otherwise | not kept on nuPlan; the switch stays off for the nuPlan track |

Winning configuration = the kept arm with the largest difference (tie: fewer switches). Stage 2, only if an arm is kept: base and the winning
configuration on the other 791 public scenes (`ot3new`, `cf1fresh`, the lists of the existing runs), and APY10m10-AB-s0 base / winning
configuration on all 1491; same table, the 791 as the confirmation (they took no part in choosing the configuration; the control rollouts of
the replay are inside the 700). Stop rule: if no arm is kept on the 700 and the PAI table of the 20 already-seen scenes is not better than
the baseline either, stage 2 is not run.

## Guardrails for the winning configuration

- Latency: `drive` total per call in the closed-loop logs (nuPlan box and PAI Tokyo), against the base of the same run set, and the
  serving step alone; the 0.1 s target.
- HUGSIM 64 / jevdrive.bench: the switches live in the AlpaSim drivers' export step (a trajectory for AlpaSim's tracker); the bench path
  does not go through it. Reported as AlpaSim-only serving unless that turns out wrong.
- The submission image with the flags still passes `docker/test.sh`.

## Limits known in advance

One checkpoint and one rollout per scene per arm on the 700; local rendering; log-clustered intervals over 27 logs are wide. The lead limit
uses openpilot's following distance (6 m standstill gap, 1.45 s), longer than the logged drivers keep in queues: lost progress is the
expected cost and the slow-count line is there for it. nuPlan decisions are 2 Hz with six synthesised slots, where the model's speed
estimates are biased (COL1's lead speeds read about 1 m/s low); the relative-speed form openpilot uses is kept. PAI: 40 scenes, one rollout.
